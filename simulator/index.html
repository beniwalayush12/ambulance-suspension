Model

One rear corner of the ambulance: the body ms sits on a spring ks and damper cs, which sit on the wheel mu, which sits on the tyre kt. Newton's second law for each mass:

ms z̈s = −ks(zs − zu) − cs(żs − żu)(1)
mu z̈u = ks(zs − zu) + cs(żs − żu) − Ft(2)

The tyre force Ft = min{kt(zu − zr), F0}: a tyre can push on the road but cannot pull it. Ft = 0 means the wheel is off the road.

Measures and roads

  • Comfort aw: RMS body acceleration, weighted for human sensitivity (ISO 2631-1). Rough road: ISO 8608 class C, 300 m at 40 km/h.
  • Grip loss g: RMS swing of the tyre force divided by the static load F0 = (ms + mu)g. Same rough road.
  • Peak jolt: largest body acceleration over a 7 cm pothole and a 10 cm IRC:99 speed breaker at 30 km/h.

Smart damper

The damper aims for the force

F* = bs żs − bu żu(5)

and sets c = F* / (żs − żu), kept between 0.8 and 14 kN·s/m. If F* would need a push, it goes to the softest setting. The valve takes about 5 ms to respond. Study gains: bs = 16, bu = 0.8 kN·s/m.

Solved with fourth-order Runge–Kutta, 0.5 ms steps, about 78,000 steps per change. This page uses the same equations, road profile and settings as the slides and reproduces their numbers to within 0.001 m/s².
